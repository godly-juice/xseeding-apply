Applying to xSeeding: what we store, where it comes from, how to delete it

Plain words, no legalese. The code that does all of this is public: github.com/godly-juice/xseeding-apply.

What you give us

  • What you type in the form (name/handle, how to reach you, your text, your links, handles).
  • The files you drop (up to 5).
  • A hashed form of your network address (to stop spam - not the address itself).
  • Which boxes you ticked, with the exact wording you saw and the time.

Where Lumi may look - only if you tick it

  • The links you typed: we open those pages and read what is public on them.
  • Your public GitHub: profile, public repositories, README files (through GitHub's public API).
  • A web search on your name and the handles you gave: we read the public pages that come up.
  • Your uploaded files.
  • Your Google Drive, read-only - only if you click "Connect Google Drive" after sending. You can disconnect any time at myaccount.google.com > Security > Third-party access, and that ends it on our side too.

Nothing else. No logging in as you, no private messages, no friends lists, no buying data from anyone. Lumi runs on our own computer, not in a cloud service; nothing about you is sent to an outside AI provider.

What happens with it

  • Lumi writes a first summary (strengths, what you could own on the team, questions to ask). A human reads your application and that summary. The summary is not a decision.
  • Your application is seen by the owner of xSeeding and the people who help with hiring. It is never sold, shared or used for anything but this.

How long, and how to delete

  • Kept for 90 days, then deleted by itself - unless you joined the team.
  • Your delete link (shown right after you send) removes everything at once, no questions. Keep it.
  • Lost the link? Tell the person who gave you this page where you applied from, and we delete it by hand.

Who can apply

You must be 18 or older. Applying is free and never a promise of a job, money or access.

This page is written in good faith to be clear and lawful for a small team; if a law where you live gives you more rights than this (for example a right to see or correct your data), you have them here too - just ask.
